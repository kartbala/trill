Stripping wire without a stripper

Written Wednesday 30 September, 21:40, for Thursday's library session. You skipped the wire stripper at Hubo, and your Swiss Army knife can do the job.

The plan: ask the Maakcafé desk for an afstriptang first. If they have none, use the knife: the bottle-opener notch for the thick wire and the scissors for the thin wires.

2. Your knife

A red Victorinox, 91 mm long and about 3 cm thick, with seven or eight layers of tools. The photos show a magnifying glass, a Phillips screwdriver, a wood saw, a corkscrew, scissors and the toothpick. That mix points to a SwissChamp.

The photos do not show pliers. Open every tool once tonight. If you find pliers, they have a wire cutter near the hinge, which cuts the Trill cable cleanly.

Four of its tools help tomorrow:

  • Bottle opener: it has a built-in wire stripper, a small sharp notch. Victorinox's own name for it is "bottle opener with screwdriver and wire stripper".
  • Scissors: for cutting wire, and for stripping thin wire.
  • Small blade: for opening the white two-core cord.
  • Magnifying glass: for checking each solder joint.

3. Thick wire: the bottle-opener notch

For the black 1,5 mm² installation wire.

  1. Open the bottle opener and find the sharp notch at the base of the hook. Use the magnifier if you need to.
  2. Lay the wire in the notch, 1 cm from its end.
  3. Press gently and turn the wire one full circle. You are cutting the plastic, not the copper.
  4. Pull the cut piece of plastic off the end.
  5. Look at the copper. A ring cut into it means you pressed too hard: snip that end off and try again.

4. Thin wires: the scissors

For the four thin wires of the Trill cable, if you take the cable route. The notch is too big for them.

  1. Open the scissors. Put the wire between the blades, half a centimetre from the end.
  2. Close the blades just until they touch the plastic. Do not cut through.
  3. Turn the wire a little, close gently again, and repeat until the plastic is cut all the way round.
  4. Pull the cut piece off with your fingernails.
  5. Twist the copper strands together so no strand sticks out.

Go lighter than feels natural. These strands are hair-thin and break easily. If one breaks off, cut the end clean and start again: the cable has length to spare.

5. The white two-core cord

These are your limb leads. A white outer skin holds a brown and a blue wire.

  1. With the small blade, cut a slit 3 cm long down the white skin. Cut away from your fingers, along the length, and not deep.
  2. Peel the skin back and cut it off with the scissors.
  3. Pull the brown and blue wires apart. Strip each end in the bottle-opener notch, as in part 3.
  4. The copper inside is many fine strands. Twist each end tight and melt a little solder onto it before you join it to anything.

6. Before you strip anything that matters

  • Practise twice on a spare end of the black installation wire.
  • Close each tool before you open the next one. The blade does not lock.
  • After soldering, check each joint under the magnifier. A good joint is shiny and shaped like a small volcano, with no gap around the wire.
